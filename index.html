06 FAQ

Before you
strike a chord.

A few things you might like to know before your first session.

Which instruments can I tune with TuneForge?

Guitar, bass, ukulele and mandolin each have their own tuning library. For anything else, Chromatic mode shows the nearest note to whatever you play.

Can I use alternate or custom tunings?

Yes. Alongside Standard you’ll find dropped tunings such as Drop D and Drop C, alternate tunings like DADGAD and Open C, down-tuned options and extended-range tunings. You can also build your own: move each string by a semitone or an octave, add or remove strings, and save it to your library.

What’s in the musician’s toolkit?

Six tools: a metronome with subdivisions, tap tempo and a speed trainer; a fret calculator; a reference drone; a plain-string tension tool; a transposer for playing with a capo; and a chord library.

Why does TuneForge need my microphone?

The tuner listens to your instrument to work out which note you’re playing and how close it is to pitch. Sound is analysed in real time on your device — it isn’t recorded or uploaded. You can read more in our Privacy Policy.

Can I change how TuneForge looks?

Yes. Choose from ten accent colours — Green, Turquoise, Sky, Blue, Violet, Rose, Coral, Amber, Lime and Silver — pick your launcher icon, and switch the instrument grid background on or off.

Where can I get TuneForge?

TuneForge is coming soon to Android. As soon as it’s available, the download buttons on this page will take you straight to Google Play.

Does this website track me?

No. This site doesn’t use cookies, analytics or tracking of any kind, and our fonts and images are served from the site itself. Our Privacy Policy has the details.
